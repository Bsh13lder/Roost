Roost avatars, round six: soft bodies, lens eyes

A soft, squeezable body you could hold in one hand, and a pair of camera lenses that do all the feeling. The bodies stay simple: smooth, rounded, no limbs and no mouth. The machine soul lives in the lenses: irises that dilate and focus with a mechanical tick, shutters that close at an angle, a face that tilts and a body that squashes. Four directions below, ten silhouettes each. Move your pointer: every bot looks your way, and one you come close to zooms its lenses on you. The app does not change until a direction is picked.

The design language, and what we stay away from

Soft body, lens soul

Every body is one smooth closed curve (a superellipse with a few gentle dents or bumps), shaded like matte vinyl. The face is two camera lenses: a dark glass, a coloured iris with a focus ring, a pupil and a hard highlight. Feeling comes from shutters, irises, tilt and squash, never from a mouth.

Cinematic, not anime

Motion with weight: anticipation, overshoot, settle. Light, not symbols. No sweat drops, no sparkle eyes, no blush, no ^^ eyes, no "?" or "z" glyphs. A status is a posture plus a tiny LED.

Clean room

No binocular head on a neck, no egg body with glowing LED eyes, no film-robot look-alike of any kind. No irregular blob with two vertical pill eyes. Our eyes are round lenses with shutters and irises; our bodies are regular, symmetric curves.

Small stays still

Under 32 px a bot only blinks. prefers-reduced-motion and the Calm robots setting leave only slow blinks everywhere. One shared frame loop moves the eyes and pauses when the tab is hidden or a bot is off screen.

Playground pick · then press an animation

Pick a direction, a shape, a colour and a status, then press any animation. The big bot and its four neighbours each follow your pointer on their own: come close and their lenses zoom; pass fast and the nearest one does a double-take. Hover to make one lean in, click to poke it. Random life plays the idle variety by itself.

The four directions shapes · colours · statuses · star · picture

For each: ten silhouettes at the picker's 56 px, six colours at 96, 40 and 24 px, the five statuses at 72, 40 and 24 px, the main bot's star, and an uploaded picture inside the shape, with and without its lenses.

In context sidebar · hero · chat · avatar card

The cozy-kit pieces the bots live in: pinned tiles (3 per row, 64 px, with names), the Profile's panel hero, the chat header pill, and the avatar card. Switch the direction to compare.

Performance and accessibility

One shared requestAnimationFrame loop owns the pointer and every live bot. It reads the pointer once per frame, caches each bot's box until scroll or resize, and only touches bots an IntersectionObserver reports on screen (the others get their CSS animations paused). It stops when the tab is hidden, when nothing moves, and when motion is turned down. Per frame it writes four CSS variables (--ex, --ey for the lenses, --lx, --lr for the lean); every other motion is a CSS animation on a class or an attribute. Every bot is one role="img" with its name and status; the LED, the pulse ring and the glint are decoration.

Calm robotsBots keep still: no following, no idle life, no events. They only blink, slowly.

Recommendation which direction and why

Pick Peeper. It is the closest to what you love about soft avatars (one calm, squeezable silhouette with a small, simple face) while giving the lenses the most room to act. Two separate porthole lenses, each with its own shutter, can do every emotion on the list by themselves: the shutters slope for sad, rise from below for a satisfied squint, part wide for a startle, and close one at a time for a curious squint, while the irises dilate on notice and narrow to focus. Nothing sits between the eyes, so it reads at 24 px as "two lenses on a pebble", which no other product's avatar looks like.

How this maps to code

It plugs into the robot engine we already have (web/src/components/robot/): SVG plus CSS animations, no new dependency, no new loop.

lookAt.ts · useLookAt.ts

Unchanged in shape: the one module-level loop still writes --ex/--ey/--lx/--lr, caches boxes, pauses off screen and when hidden, fires the double-take, the look-around and the idle life by flashing robot--ev-* classes. Two additions: a notice event (the pointer comes within 1.6 bot-widths: iris zoom, once per approach) and whirr in the idle list. faceTracks also follows while the face is focus (On it). LIVE_OVER_PX stays 32.

moments.ts · events.ts

RobotFaceLids becomes open · wide · focus · narrow · sad · happy · closed; MOMENT_FACE maps On it → focus and Stuck → sad. New events notice, whirr, power (the slow power down when a bot goes Off duty) and boot join EVENT_MS; done sets the happy face. events.ts adds one rule: a bot_status frame into off_duty fires power.

shapes/peeper.ts (new, replaces ghost.ts)

The ten silhouettes as a table of a few numbers each (width, height, top and bottom roundness, taper, lean, dents, tilt) and one pure bodyPath(shape) that samples 48 points and returns the smooth path, its box, the eye line and the lens radius. Same point count for every shape, so the picker can still morph.

RobotFace.tsx · eyes.tsx · fx.tsx

RobotFace keeps its props (shape, color, moment, star, picture, pictureEyes, size, still, events) and the groups the motion needs: pose · float · evt · lean · tilter. eyes.tsx draws one lens at radius 10 (glass, iris with focus ring, pupil, highlights, scan glint, two shutters in one clip) and scales it into place. fx.tsx shrinks to the LED and the pulse ring.

robot.css · tokens.css

The face variables (--lt --la --lbt --lba --is --lz --ft --fy), the status loops and the event keyframes above, scoped by html:not(.calm) and .robot--live. Glass, LED amber and star colours join tokens.css.

look.ts

New names go in PEEPER_SHAPES. Nothing stored migrates: the ten ghost names and every older shape draw through one table, shape → peeper, below. roost/constants.py, api/types.ts and tests/test_bot_look.py move in step.
