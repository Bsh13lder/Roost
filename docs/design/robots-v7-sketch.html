Roost avatars, round seven: Lensbot and Peeper

A deeper pass on the two families you picked. No mouths and no glow: every bot has clean white eyes with dark pupils and a small highlight, and each shape has its own eye shape. Seven eye styles to choose from, eighteen Lensbots and fifteen Peepers. The eyes also carry information: a "?" when a bot is asking you, an "!" when it's stuck, a spinner while it works, a check when it's done. Move your pointer and every bot looks at you. Nothing in the app changes until you pick.

The rules for this round

White eyes, no glow

Solid white sclera, a dark pupil and a small hard highlight. A faint shade under the top edge makes it feel like a real eye. No glow, no blur, no coloured light in the eyes. Lensbot's eyes sit in a dark visor; Peeper's sit in a bezel set into the body.

No mouth, ever

All the feeling comes from the lids (tilt, squint, droop), the pupils (dilate, pinch, dart), the lens zoom and how the body moves. Our shapes and eyes are our own: regular, symmetric curves and seven geometric eye shapes.

Info lives in the eye field

The bot's need is a small dark glyph drawn inside the eye: it replaces the pupil in one eye, or both pupils while working. Glyphs read at 40 px. Below 32 px a tiny coloured dot in the corner says the same thing.

Cinematic, not anime

Motion with weight: anticipation, overshoot, settle. Lenses focus, irises zoom, lids tilt. No sparkle eyes, no blush, no sweat drops, no ^^ eyes. Calm robots and reduced motion keep the glyph but stop it moving, and leave only slow blinks.

Playground family · shape · eyes · colour · state · animation

Pick a family, a shape, an eye style, a colour and a state, then press an animation. "Shape's own" uses the eye style that shape ships with. The big bot and its four neighbours each follow your pointer: come close and their lenses focus on you, pass by quickly and the nearest one does a double-take. Hover to make one lean in, click to poke it.

Info in the eye field at 64 · 40 · 24 px

Each state as Lensbot and Peeper at 64 and 40 px, then at 24 px, where only the coloured dot shows. Glyphs are dark ink on the white eye, so each reads as a shape and not a colour: "?" for asking, "!" for stuck, a cursor for take control, an envelope for a new message, a check for done. On it turns both pupils into a spinner (Peeper) or a scanning bar (Lensbot), and thinking sends three dots across both eyes.

The two families shapes · eyes · colours · states · star · picture

For each family: every silhouette at the picker's 56 px, shown with its own eye style. Then the seven eye styles on two shapes, six colours at 96, 40 and 24 px, all nine states, the main bot's star, and an uploaded picture inside the body.

In context sidebar · hero · chat · avatar card

The cozy-kit pieces the bots live in: pinned tiles (3 per row, 64 px, with names), the Profile's panel hero, the chat header pill (24 px, with its dot cue), and the avatar card with a Shape tab and an Eyes tab. The card works: switch tabs, then pick a shape or an eye style.

Performance and accessibility

One shared requestAnimationFrame loop owns the pointer and every live bot. It reads the pointer once per frame, caches each bot's box until scroll or resize, and only touches bots an IntersectionObserver reports on screen (the others get their CSS animations paused). It stops when the tab is hidden, when nothing moves, and when motion is turned down. Each frame it writes four CSS variables (--ex, --ey for the pupils, --lx, --lr for the lean). Everything else is a CSS animation on a class or an attribute. Each eye style sets how far its pupil travels (--pkx, --pky), so the loop stays the same for every shape. Every bot is one role="img" named with its name and state (for example "Scout, Asking you"), so a glyph is never the only signal. The pill next to the bot says it in words too.

Calm robotsBots keep still: no following, no idle life, no events. Glyphs stay, but they don't move. Blinks only, slowly.

Recommendation what to build

Build Lensbot first, and give Peeper the same eyes as a second family. The dark visor is what makes this round work. It frames two white eyes with high contrast on every body colour, so the pupils and the glyphs read at 40 px even on Butter or Stone, where a white eye on a pale body loses its edge. The visor also turns a whole-face tilt into one clear gesture, and because its frame varies (band, pill, goggle, screen, brow, mask), eighteen Lensbots still look like eighteen different bots. Peeper is the softer, friendlier family. It shares the eye renderer, the glyphs and the motion, so it costs one shape table and a bezel drawer, and can ship right after Lensbot without new engine work.

How this maps to code

It plugs into the robot engine we already have (web/src/components/robot/): SVG plus CSS animations, with no new dependency and no new loop.

lookAt.ts · useLookAt.ts

The same module-level loop still writes --ex/--ey/--lx/--lr, caches boxes, pauses off screen and when the tab is hidden, and fires the double-take, the look-around and idle life by flashing robot--ev-* classes. Additions: a notice event (the pointer comes within 1.6 bot-widths: lens zoom and pupil dilate, once per approach), whirr and hmm in the idle list, and no tracking while both pupils are replaced by a glyph. LIVE_OVER_PX stays 32: below it a bot only blinks and shows its dot cue.

moments.ts · events.ts

RobotFaceLids becomes open · wide · focus · narrow · sad · happy · closed. A new RobotGlyph (onit · think · ask · stuck · ctrl · msg · done · off) has one table from status to glyph and one from live frames to short-lived glyphs. In events.ts: the thinking set gives think, a new unread reply flashes msg, a finished run flashes done plus a satisfied blink, a Stuck card waiting on Take control gives ctrl, and a bot_status frame into off_duty fires power, which ends on off. Creating a bot fires the created hop.

shapes/lensbot.ts · shapes/peeper.ts (new, replace ghost.ts)

Each silhouette is a row of a few numbers (width, height, top and bottom roundness, taper, lean, dents and bumps, ears, feet, a nub), plus its own eye style and its visor or bezel. One pure bodyPath(shape) samples 48 points and returns the path, the box and the face layout. Same point count for every shape, so the picker can morph between them.

RobotFace.tsx · eyes.tsx · glyphs.tsx · fx.tsx

RobotFace keeps its props and gains eyes and glyph. eyes.tsx draws one eye per style in eye units: white sclera, pupil and highlight, two lids in a group scaled to the eye's height, so every face number works for every style. glyphs.tsx holds the eight glyphs as tiny paths, only for bots at 32 px and up. Below that it draws the corner dot. fx.tsx keeps the pulse ring and the off-duty z.

avatar JSON · api/types.ts · roost/constants.py

A new optional eyes field on the avatar: "round" | "oval" | "tall" | "square" | "half" | "almond" | "ring". Missing or null means the shape's own eyes, so every stored avatar stays valid and nothing migrates. The avatar JSON is already plaintext (look only, never user content). The gateway validates it with a pydantic Literal from EYE_STYLES in roost/constants.py, templates (.roost-bot.json) carry it, and AvatarLook in look.ts sends it from the avatar card.

robot.css · tokens.css

The face variables (--lt --la --lbt --lba --is --lz --ft --fy), the glyph rules, the status loops and the event keyframes above, scoped by html:not(.calm) and .robot--live. The sclera white, pupil ink, visor and dot-cue colours join tokens.css. Calm and reduced motion keep glyphs visible but still.

look.ts

New names go in LENSBOT_SHAPES and PEEPER_SHAPES, plus an EYE_STYLES list and eyesFor(shape, eyes). Names are unique across both families and every old name, so the family is known from the name alone. Old saved shapes draw through one table, shape → lensbot, shown below. roost/constants.py, api/types.ts and tests/test_bot_look.py change together.
