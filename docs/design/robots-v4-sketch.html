Roost avatars, round four

Chick stays as the base, now with eyes only and a lot more life: the eyes follow your pointer, the bots breathe, blink and glance about, and each thing that happens to a bot shows on its face and in its body. Two more families join it, Penguin and Ghost. Move your pointer across the page and every bot on it looks your way. The app does not change until this round is picked.

What changed, and what we stay away from

Eyes only

No mouth and no beak. The face is two round ink eyes with a highlight. Feeling comes from the eyes (shape, squint, size, lids, sparkle), optional blush, and how the body moves.

Alive, but calm

Eyes follow the pointer, clamped inside the eye. At rest a bot breathes, blinks at its own pace, glances about and wiggles once in a while. On hover it leans to you; a click squishes it (no sound).

Our own look

Named, mirrored shapes on one 48-unit grid and one perch line. Not irregular blobs, not vertical pill eyes, and no borrowed character's proportions. Three families: Chick, Penguin, Ghost.

Small stays still

Under 32 px only blinks remain: no pointer tracking, no hops. prefers-reduced-motion and the Calm robots setting turn tracking, bobs and hops off everywhere and keep slow blinks.

Playground pick · then press an event

Pick a family, a shape and a colour, set a status, then press an event. The big bot and its four neighbours each follow your pointer on their own. Hover one to pet it, click to poke it.

A · Three families eyes only

Each family has 8 to 14 shapes, so a team of bots can all look different, and every shape takes any of the ten colours. Bodies keep round three's soft light from the upper left, a gloss and a ground shadow.

B · In context hero · sidebar · picker

The same three cozy-kit pieces for each family: the Profile's panel hero, the sidebar with its pinned row, and the avatar picker card with its Robot / Upload / Reset tabs, the shape grid and the colour dots.

C · Performance and accessibility

One shared requestAnimationFrame loop moves every eye on the page. It reads the pointer once per frame, keeps each bot's box in a cache that scroll and resize invalidate, and only touches bots an IntersectionObserver says are on screen. It stops when the tab is hidden, when nothing is on screen, and when motion is turned down. Each eye is two CSS variables (--ex, --ey) and the hover lean two more (--lx, --lr); the browser does the rest in CSS.

Every decorative part (the "?" sparkle, the z, the sparks) is aria-hidden; the whole bot is one role="img" with its name and status. prefers-reduced-motion turns off tracking, the bob and the hops and keeps only slow blinks. The same switch is a setting:

Calm robotsBots keep still: no eye following, no hops. They only blink, slowly.

D · How this maps to code

Everything stays SVG plus CSS animations, with no new dependency. A bot is one stateful SVG: data-s for its status, data-face for its eyes, a class for an event in flight. The status motions, the event motions and the face swaps are all CSS rules on those attributes, so a status change is an attribute flip, not a redraw. The only JavaScript that runs per frame is the small shared pointer loop.

web/src/components/robot/RobotFace.tsx

Draws the body for the shape's family (Chick, Penguin or Ghost), the face, the glints, the sparks and the star, inside the four groups the motion needs: pose (status posture), anim (status and event motion), poke (the click squish) and lean (the hover). Props: shape, color, moment, star, picture, pictureEyes, size, still, events.

shapes/chick.ts · penguin.ts · ghost.ts · eyes.tsx

Each family is a data table of a few numbers per shape (width, height, squareness, taper, flat base, and a feature) plus one pure path function, as in round three. eyes.tsx draws one eye with all its faces (open, wide, happy, closed, squint, dizzy) in one group; CSS shows one. The blush, the "?" sparkle, the z and the sparks come from glints.tsx.

useLookAt.ts

A small hook: useLookAt(ref, { eyeY, enabled }) registers the SVG with one module-level loop (lookAt.ts) that owns the pointer, the box cache, the IntersectionObserver and the visibilitychange pause. It writes --ex, --ey, --lx and --lr on the element and nothing else. Disabled below 32 px, when still is set, and when a face is not open.

events prop

events: readonly RobotEvent[] (created · think · done · msg · stuck · ask · off, each with a key). A new key adds the event class for its length, then restores the status face. The chat feeds it from live bot_status changes (On it → Resting with a write tool result is done; a create_* result is created; a new reply is msg) and the card routes (Stuck, Ask).

look.ts

Shape ids gain a family prefix (chick-egg, penguin-classic, ghost-wisp). Old saved shapes still draw through look.ts's table (below): the bare bird and Boxbot names map onto Chick shapes, and stored avatars keep their value. roost/constants.py, api/types.ts and tests/test_bot_look.py move in step.

robot.css · tokens.css · palette.ts

The keyframes above, scoped by html:not(.calm) and .robot--live. Ink, blush, star and ground-shadow tokens join tokens.css. The Calm robots setting and prefers-reduced-motion both set still; tests see it as robot--still. Bundle: no new dependencies; the family tables are a few hundred bytes each.
